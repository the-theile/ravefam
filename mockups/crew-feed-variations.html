Crew Feed — four visual takes

Same structure in all four: Huddle strip → merged Who's Going / Rave Plan ticket → filter chips → one feed of polls, dreams, plan check-offs, jams, pins and archive drops. Only the visual language changes. Each one borrows from something RaveFAM already has. Filter chips work in every phone.

1 · Backstage Pass

Every update is a ticket stub

Leans into the crew-pass / laminate identity. Cream paper cards with a neon stub on the left naming the type, dashed perforations, mono labels, a red "NEW" ink stamp. The ticket gets a real barcode strip. Huddle reads like a radio channel.

Feels: tactile, collectible, very "RaveFAM". Watch: lots of cream on a dark app is bright; it may need to tone down to fewer paper cards (e.g. only plan + pins on paper).
2 · Aurora Glass

Soft, blurred, social-app polish

Neon becomes a blurred aurora behind frosted-glass cards. Big round avatars lead each post, the type icon sits in a tinted circle, actions are pill chips. The ticket is a glass card with a checklist progress ring; Huddle is a pill with a typing indicator.

Feels: modern and friendly; the most like a social app. Watch: backdrop blur costs performance on older phones; it needs a solid fallback.
3 · Flyer Wall

Posters taped to a wall

Pulls from the Gig Poster share card. Flat neon blocks with black type, hard white offset shadows, slight tilts, "NEW!" stickers. The countdown becomes a giant "20". Huddle is a yellow sticker strip. Loud on purpose.

Feels: energetic, flyer culture, very shareable. Watch: six full neon fills at once is a lot; long feeds may tire the eye.
4 · Comms Log

No cards, just a clean timeline

Matches the Huddle's "festival comms channel" style. No card boxes: a thin vertical line with colored nodes, mono timestamps on the left, Today / Earlier dividers. Huddle is a one-line live ticker; the ticket is outlined with corner brackets.

Feels: calm, dense, fast to scan; the most room for content. Watch: least "fun"; relies on color and type alone for personality.
